A Little Background About Myself

I’m Bianca, a 19-year-old college student who is still discovering different sides of herself. I believe that growing up is a continuous process filled with new experiences, lessons, challenges, and meaningful memories.

I created this website to look back on the different stages of my life and understand how each experience has contributed to the person I am today. Through this journey, I hope to appreciate not only how much I’ve changed but also how much I still have to learn and discover about myself.

Every chapter has shaped me, and this is where my story unfolds. ♡

My Beginning: Prenatal Development and Infancy (Conception to 2 Years)

When my mom was pregnant with me, she told me that she experienced some complications because her asthma came back. Interestingly, she didn’t experience any complications when she was pregnant with my older sister. She also shared that she almost died while giving birth to me. Her body even turned violet during labor, which made my family extremely worried. Because of that experience, my family became hesitant to have another child.

According to my mom, I was a very well-behaved baby. I rarely cried in the middle of the night, so I didn’t give my parents much trouble when it came to taking care of me. In short, I was a good baby! HAHAHA!

As for my early development, I started walking when I was around one year old and began talking at approximately two years old. My mom also told me that I was comfortable around other people even when I was still a baby.

My Childhood Years (Early and Middle Childhood)

figure>

A few moments from my childhood.

The Little Me

I have a lot of good childhood memories, like wearing my bathing suit and waiting for the rain to come, or playing pretend by myself and with my friends. There are so many more, but one of my funniest yet most tragic memories happened when I was six years old.

I was playing outside with my cousins when they invited me to ride their three-wheeled bicycle. They said, “Let’s go for a stroll!” so I happily hopped on. While my cousin was driving, he suddenly said, “We’re going to the cemetery!” Obviously, I got scared because they were talking about visiting our deceased grandparents. Out of fear, I jumped off the bicycle while we were on a rocky road filled with stones. Unfortunately, I landed badly, and my arm was bleeding. I had an open cut on my elbow.

I walked home crying while my cousins started to panic because they didn’t know what to do. When my mom saw me, she immediately panicked because she had no idea what had happened. Until now, I still laugh whenever I remember that incident because, honestly, it’s so funny when I think about it. I guess that sums up how dramatic I have been since I was a child. HAHAHA!

Speaking of being dramatic, I was also a very talkative child, and honestly, I still am! I remember that when I was in kindergarten, my teacher would tell my mom that I was always roaming around the classroom and talking to my classmates as if I were their supervisor. That’s actually how I got the nickname “Supervisor.”

When it came to socializing, I was a very extroverted child. I wasn’t shy around new people, even if I had never met them before. I would always try to approach them, start conversations, and invite them to play. Making friends came naturally to me when I was younger.

However, despite being outgoing, I was also very sensitive, especially when someone raised their voice at me. Whenever that happened, I would instantly start crying. Even when I tried my best to hold back my tears, I just couldn’t help it.

I also remember having good leadership skills as a child. Whenever we had school events, I would often be chosen as the dance leader. I also loved singing! Back then, my teacher would sometimes ask me to sing in front of the class whenever she felt too lazy to teach us. HAHAHA! She probably thought my voice was good enough to entertain the class.

Looking back, these little experiences make me smile because they remind me of the kind of child I was: talkative, playful, outgoing, sensitive, and always ready to take the lead. Although I’ve grown older, I can still recognize some of these qualities in the person I am today.

My Teenage Years: Discovering Myself (Adolescence)

A few moments from my teenage years.

When I was in Grade 7, most of my friends were already going through puberty, while I hadn’t experienced many physical changes yet. I got my first period when I was in Grade 8, at the age of 13. It was also during the pandemic.

When I turned 13, a lot of things started changing for me. I became more conscious of my weight and appearance because I had been bullied before for being fat and having tanned skin. I still remember one specific experience from Grade 7. I had a crush on a boy from my school, but he didn’t like me back because he told me that he liked my sister more. Of course, that hurt my feelings because I always thought my sister was prettier than me and had fairer skin.

Eventually, I got tired of the constant teasing from my classmates. I used those experiences as motivation to work on myself. I started exercising and eating healthier foods. After a few months, my appearance changed, and I began to appreciate the new version of myself. When I posted a picture online, people were surprised because they barely recognized me. Even that boy I used to have a crush on replied to my story and started messaging me, saying that he liked me now. But guess what? I didn’t like him back anymore. HAHAHA!

Despite those experiences, I had a good relationship with my friends. Although I’ve lost a few of them over the years, I guess that’s just how life works, right? I can honestly say that I had some of the best teenage years because my friends made sure that we enjoyed our high school experience. We would often go home late because we loved going out, strolling around, and eating together.

One of my favorite memories was from Grade 10. I joined my first pageant, and my classmates cheered for me throughout the entire competition. I ended up winning first place, and I was extremely happy and proud of myself. It was one of the experiences that helped boost my confidence.

After graduating from junior high school, I moved on to senior high school. It was a big adjustment for me because I had to deal with a new environment and meet new people. Fortunately, I made friends and continued doing well academically. I also actively participated in school events and pageants.

However, not everything went smoothly. During our final term in the second semester, my classmates and I had an issue with some of our teachers because we felt that they weren’t teaching us properly. Instead of discussing the lessons with us, they would often use their phones and assign us to teach our classmates through classroom reports. As students, we felt that this was unfair, especially since we were studying in a private school.

Things became more frustrating when our teachers announced that we would have an examination the following day, even though we felt that we hadn’t been properly taught the lessons. Because of this, my classmates and I decided to raise our concerns with the school head.

Our teachers became upset with us. They confronted and scolded us because of what we had done. After the incident, I became distant from some of my classmates because I felt that they had betrayed our trust. It became difficult for me to trust people again.

That experience taught me an important lesson about trust, communication, and standing up for what I believe is right. At the same time, I also felt bad for my teachers because they blamed us for their decision to consider resigning instead of staying at the school. I felt guilty, but I also knew that we were simply asking for the education we deserved. If we had been properly taught and given the guidance we needed, we wouldn’t have felt the need to complain in the first place.

As much as I wanted to enjoy the last few months of senior high school, I found it difficult because of everything that had happened. Still, I’m grateful for the experiences I had at my old school. Despite the challenges, the people I met, the memories I made, and the lessons I learned all helped shape the person I am today.

My Present Self: Emerging Adulthood or My Current Developmental Stage

Me today.

All I can say is that my present self is different from my teenage self because of everything I’ve experienced and all the lessons I’ve learned from friendships, relationships, and life in general.

I am now a second-year college student studying Psychology. Despite all the changes I’ve gone through, I still have the same personality as before. I’m still very talkative!

When it comes to my physical health, I try to eat as healthily as I can to maintain my body. However, I realized that some of my habits are still influenced by my past experiences with bullying. I’m still scared of gaining weight because I don’t want to experience being bullied again. I know that I need to improve my relationship with food. I want to remind myself that it’s okay to eat and that I don’t have to feel guilty after eating. I hope that, as time goes by, I can become gentler with myself and develop a healthier relationship with food.

When it comes to friendships, I have met new people who genuinely treat me well. They value friendship just as much as I do. Our friendship isn’t perfect, but we’re happy, and most importantly, we help and support one another. My first year of college was a big adjustment for me because it was my first time studying so far away from home. Imagine having to wake up at 4 a.m. every day because my university is about an hour and a half away from home when commuting. Not gonna lie, it was very draining. But when you have good friends, they give you more reasons to look forward to going to college. There’s never a day when I don’t laugh whenever I’m with them. HAHAHA! They’re just that type of friends. I didn’t just find a group of friends; I found a sisterhood.

One thing I’ve noticed about myself is that I’ve become more cautious when it comes to friendships. For example, I used to be comfortable hanging out with people I didn’t know, such as my friends’ other friends. But I’m not like that anymore. I realized that it’s okay to have fewer friends who are genuine and loyal to you rather than having a lot of friends who aren’t sincere. Quality over quantity, right? HAHAHA!

When it comes to relationships, I’ve also realized that you don’t have to shrink yourself just to be loved properly. I’ve learned to love myself more compared to how I did before. After all, how can you give love to someone else if you don’t know how to love and value yourself? Nowadays, I make sure to prioritize myself and my well-being. I met someone last year when I thought I would never be able to love again because of my past experiences. But now, we’re officially together! He has helped me appreciate and love myself even more. He’s one of the most important people in my life right now. With him, I have the freedom to express myself without constantly overthinking whether he’ll judge me or not. He has helped me heal in so many ways. I love that I feel at peace now. Honestly, my life feels so much better. My past relationships helped shape who I am today. All those challenges and difficult experiences helped make me stronger.

Academically, I initially thought that studying Psychology would be boring, but I was completely wrong! I’m actually enjoying it, even though it can be challenging sometimes. HAHAHA! I find myself cramming a lot because college comes with so many requirements and responsibilities. It’s definitely different from grade school and high school. Studying Psychology has also influenced my decision-making, especially now that I’m learning more about human behavior. I understand better why people act the way they do. I’ve also noticed that I’ve become better at listening to other people’s problems. Unlike before, when I didn’t really know how to comfort someone, I can now listen and try to understand what they’re going through. I’m still working on this skill, but at least there’s progress! Psychology has really contributed to the person I’m becoming, and I’m very proud of that.

When I think about my future, I see myself building a career in the field of Psychology. This is one of the reasons why I always try my best academically. I know that achieving my goals won’t always be easy, but I want to give myself the opportunities and future that I deserve. I want to become someone who is successful, independent, and happy with the life I’ve built for myself.

Connecting My Experiences to Developmental Theories

Our development is influenced by different experiences, relationships, and environments as we grow older. Looking back at my infancy, childhood, teenage years, and present life, I can see how different experiences have shaped the person I am today. Two developmental theories that I can relate to my personal experiences are Erik Erikson’s Psychosocial Development Theory and Urie Bronfenbrenner’s Ecological Systems Theory.

Erik Erikson’s Psychosocial Development Theory

Theory / concept
Initiative vs. Guilt, Industry vs. Inferiority, Identity vs. Role Confusion, and Intimacy vs. Isolation
Brief explanation
Erik Erikson’s Psychosocial Development Theory explains how individuals develop their personalities and sense of identity through eight stages of life (Erikson, 1963). According to Erikson, each stage involves a psychosocial conflict or challenge that individuals must learn to navigate. How they handle these challenges can influence their emotional and social development.
Personal experience and how it applies

I can relate this theory to different experiences throughout my life. During my childhood, I was a playful, talkative, and outgoing child. I enjoyed roleplaying, approaching other children, and participating in school activities. I was also often chosen as a dance leader and was encouraged by my teacher to sing in front of the class. These experiences relate to Erikson’s stages of Initiative vs. Guilt and Industry vs. Inferiority because I was given opportunities to explore my interests, take initiative, develop my abilities, and gain confidence in what I could do.

During my teenage years, I experienced Identity vs. Role Confusion, which focuses on discovering one’s identity, values, and sense of self. I became more conscious of my appearance because of the bullying and comparisons I experienced. I also started making changes in my life and gradually developed a better understanding of myself. Although I struggled with insecurities, those experiences helped me recognize my worth and become more confident.

As I entered young adulthood, I began experiencing Erikson’s stage of Intimacy vs. Isolation. This stage focuses on developing meaningful and close relationships while maintaining one’s sense of identity. I can relate this to my present life because I have learned to value genuine friendships and build relationships with people who support and respect me. I also entered a romantic relationship where I feel comfortable expressing myself without being afraid of judgment. These experiences have taught me that I can build meaningful connections without losing my identity.

How the theory helps me understand my development

Overall, Erikson’s theory helps me understand how my experiences at different stages of life contributed to my personality, confidence, identity, and relationships. It reminds me that personal development continues as I grow older.

Urie Bronfenbrenner’s Ecological Systems Theory

Theory / concept
Microsystem (family, friends, school), and the Chronosystem
Brief explanation
Urie Bronfenbrenner’s Ecological Systems Theory explains how a person’s development is influenced by the different environments and people surrounding them (Bronfenbrenner, 1979). According to this theory, individuals do not develop on their own. Their family, friends, school, community, culture, and significant life experiences can all influence who they become.
Personal experience and how it applies

I can relate this theory to my own life because many of my experiences were influenced by the people around me. One example is my family, especially my mother, who shared stories about my infancy and early development. My family played an important role in my early life by taking care of me and providing the environment in which I grew up.

Another example is my school environment and friendships. During my teenage years, I experienced bullying from some of my classmates, which affected how I viewed myself and my appearance. However, I also had friends who made my high school years memorable and helped me enjoy my adolescence. These experiences demonstrate how the people in my immediate environment can influence my emotional and social development.

My senior high school experience also showed me how my school environment could affect me. The conflict between my classmates and teachers became a difficult experience that influenced how I viewed trust and relationships. On the other hand, when I entered college, I met friends who made my adjustment easier. Even though I had to wake up early and travel a long distance to attend university, their support made my college experience more enjoyable.

The Chronosystem is also relevant to my development because it focuses on how changes and significant events over time influence a person’s life. For example, the COVID-19 pandemic happened during my adolescence and changed how I experienced school and social interactions. Another example is my transition from high school to college, which required me to adjust to a new environment, new responsibilities, and new relationships.

How the theory helps me understand my development

Through Bronfenbrenner’s theory, I realized that my development was not influenced by my own decisions and experiences alone. The people I met, the environments I grew up in, and the changes I experienced throughout my life also helped shape who I am today.

Reflection: The Person I Am Becoming

After learning about these two theories, I realized that my development is a combination of the personal challenges I experienced and the environments that influenced me. Erikson’s theory helped me understand how I developed my identity and learned to build meaningful relationships, while Bronfenbrenner’s theory helped me recognize the influence of my family, friends, school, and life experiences.

Both theories remind me that development is a continuous process. I may have changed in many ways since childhood, but every experience has contributed to the person I am becoming. I am still learning, growing, and discovering more about myself, and I know that my development does not stop here.
